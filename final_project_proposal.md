We plan to investigate: To what extent do rhythm-related and structural musical features improve audio-based piano performance difficulty prediction beyond conventional timbral and spectral features? Our system takes solo piano audio recordings only (without any symbolic scores) as input and outputs a continuous real-valued difficulty score treated as a relative ordering. For development, we will use the PSyllabus dataset, where ground-truth difficulty is defined as an ordinal variable across 11 discrete pedagogical grades (0–10) without assuming uniform distances between levels. For our baseline experiment, we will use Constant-Q Transform (CQT) representations (precomputed in PSyllabus or extracted directly from WAV audio). To address this ordinal regression task, we will use two formulations that both yield continuous ranking scores: Ridge Regression or Ordinal Logistic Regression.

We will then extract audio-derived rhythm-related features, including onset density and tempogram statistics, and conduct feature-group ablation experiments to assess their contribution beyond the baseline CQT features. If time permits, we will extend the system with audio-based structural features derived from chroma self-similarity matrices (SSM), such as repetition-related statistics. The baseline, rhythm features, and ablation experiments will constitute the core project.&#x20;

Kendall’s Tau-c—which evaluates rank agreement without assuming fixed interval distances between categories—will serve as our primary metric. For model selection on PSyllabus, we will additionally report Spearman’s correlation, MSE, MAE, and Accuracy within one level (Acc±1). All methods will use the same composer-disjoint train–validation–test split, keeping recordings and excerpts of the same piece together and tuning models exclusively on the validation set. Finally, we will conduct an error analysis on misranked pieces to identify system limitations.
